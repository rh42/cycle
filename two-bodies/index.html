Two bodies,
one month.

Estrogen and testosterone run in every body, all month long. What differs is how much, and whether the amount moves by the hour or by the week. Here is one month of each, in a woman and in a man. First the way charts usually draw it. Then on the same scale.

What they are

Two small molecules. Most people know them as the female hormone and the male hormone. Both bodies make both: through the reproductive years the ovaries make most of the estradiol in her blood and the testes most of the testosterone in his, but fat, skin, brain and the adrenal glands make or convert both, in everyone.

They are nearly the same thing: four rings of carbon with an oxygen at each end. Testosterone has one extra carbon. One enzyme, aromatase, removes it and turns testosterone into estradiol. Every body that has one has the other. Estradiol is the main estrogen in a body that cycles, and the one the chart shows.

What they do

What they do overlaps. Estradiol builds the lining of the uterus and drives the monthly cycle. It also keeps skin supple, and it keeps bone dense and blood vessels flexible in men as well. Testosterone contributes to muscle, bone, body hair and desire, in women as well.

Do they run your mood? For most people, not much. Across 47 studies that tracked people day by day the findings do not line up: about one in seven found the premenstrual link people expect, and more than twice as many found no link to any phase at all. For about three in a hundred, fewer by the strictest count and more by looser ones, the ordinary rise and fall reliably triggers severe symptoms. Their hormone levels are not unusual, but their brains are more sensitive to the change.

Neither one is flat

His testosterone usually drops by about a third between morning and evening, then rebuilds overnight. Hers moves in the day too, but her big change takes a week, not an evening. Same machinery. Only one of them has a month.

One hormone is half the story

In estrogen, his whole range fits under her peak-day floor. In morning testosterone, hers fits under his. A chart that shows only one hormone has already chosen a side.

Dots are blood, curves are guesses

Ranges and dots are measurements from named studies. Curves are models, drawn through group medians or generated from published rhythm and pulse figures. They show a typical shape. Every range on this page describes a group, not you.
