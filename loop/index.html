The cycle in words

This is the same story the game tells, for anyone using a screen reader or a phone.

The first half

Day 1 is the day bleeding starts, and calendars count from there. The old lining of the womb leaves over about four days. Estradiol and progesterone are low, so the pituitary, a gland at the base of the brain, sends out FSH. Follicles in the ovary start to grow, and one pulls ahead.

That follicle makes estradiol, which rebuilds the lining and holds the pituitary's LH back. Kept up for about two and a half days, the same estradiol flips it into a surge. Roughly a day and a half after the surge begins, the follicle opens and the egg comes out. That is ovulation.

This half can stretch. On average it runs from about 10 days in very short cycles to about 27 in long ones.

The second half

The empty follicle stays behind and makes progesterone, which stops the lining growing and gets it ready. This half usually lasts around 11 to 14 days and moves much less than the first.

Without a pregnancy, the empty follicle winds down. Exactly what ends it is still unknown. Progesterone falls, and the fall tells the lining to let go. The lining shrinks a little and then sheds, and the next day 1 begins.

So a late period usually means a late egg, and that was mostly decided about two weeks before anyone noticed.

If no egg comes out

Some cycles release no egg and still end in a bleed. In women with regular cycles, that bleed can come at about the usual time, so a period on its own does not prove an egg was released. How the lining breaks down in these cycles is less understood.

If an embryo implants

An egg lasts a day at most. If sperm reaches it, the embryo divides as it drifts toward the lining, and usually settles in 8 to 10 days after the egg came out. There it starts making hCG, the hormone a pregnancy test looks for.

hCG reaches the empty follicle and keeps it going, so progesterone holds up and the bleed that was due does not come. Not every embryo implants, and some that do are lost early, often before anyone knows.

The hormones

The chart draws them all as lines, but they are two different kinds of thing. Estradiol and progesterone are small molecules: four rings of carbon with an oxygen at each end. Progesterone has three more carbons.

Made by the growing follicle. It rebuilds the lining and brakes LH until, held up long enough, it flips the pituitary into a surge.

Made by the empty follicle after the egg leaves. It stops the lining growing and readies it. When it falls, the lining sheds.

Carbon in color, oxygen hollow, hydrogen as dots. Drag to turn. Shapes from the RCSB Protein Data Bank.

LH, FSH and hCG are proteins, far too large to draw this way: two chains, 203 to 237 amino acids in all. All three share the first chain. The second decides which hormone it is.

Luteinizing hormone (LH)

From the pituitary. Its surge releases the egg, and it keeps the empty follicle running afterward.

Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)

From the pituitary. It gets follicles growing early in the cycle. The chart leaves it out.

Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)

From the embryo once it implants. Its second chain is about 80% the same as LH's, and it works LH's receptor on the empty follicle. That is how it stops the clock.

What the cycle shows

The fall is the signal

After an egg, a period is caused mainly by progesterone falling. Estrogen falls too, but that is not the cause. Block progesterone's receptor in the second half of the cycle and bleeding starts within days, even while progesterone is still high in the blood.

A late period is mostly a late egg

Most of the slack sits in the first half. Across cycle lengths its average runs from 10.4 to 26.8 days against the second half's 8.0 to 12.9, and in cycles of 25 days or more, about nine in ten, the second half stays between 12.4 and 12.9. It is not rigid: averages run about 11 to 14 days depending on how they are measured, within one person they move by a day or two, and some run much shorter. The game draws 12 to 14.

A bleed does not prove an egg

In one study of women selected for regular cycles, cycles with no ovulation had a median length of 29 days against 28 for ovulatory ones, though they ranged from 20 to 37. In midlife they tend to run longer and vary far more. The game picks a day from 26 to 34. Some follicles even make progesterone without releasing an egg. Bleeding without progesterone is a different event, and a 2002 review of abnormal bleeding called it very poorly understood.

The one signal that stops the clock

If an embryo implants, usually 8 to 10 days after the egg, it makes hCG. That keeps the empty follicle alive, so progesterone holds up and the lining is not told to let go. Not every embryo implants. Without hCG nothing changes the clock, so the bleed is expected at its usual time. Some that do implant are lost early, often before anyone knows.

Time, as well as level

In 1975 Keye and Jaffe gave women estradiol injections that kept blood levels similar and varied only how long, then tested the pituitary with a dose of GnRH, the brain's signal to the pituitary. At 36 hours its LH response had all but vanished. At 60 it was bigger than usual. Two women at each time point, ten in all across the study. No surge came on its own in this experiment, even at 132 hours. Liu and Yen got one at about 58 hours, with estradiol rising in steps. The game combines the two: it holds estradiol flat and fires at 60.
